What changes at C1/C2?

Comprehension

You must follow implicit relationships, rhetorical purpose, qualification, speaker stance, local contrast and the limits of a claim—not merely identify explicit facts.

Production

You need direct task fulfilment, controlled complexity, precise vocabulary, natural cohesion, proportionate hedging and enough fluency to make the structure easy to follow under time pressure.

C-LEVEL LAB

Move from fluent to controlled

C-level performance depends on matching linguistic force to meaning: how much evidence you have, what you are conceding, what is causal, what is merely associated and which idea carries the sentence.

01

Hedging & calibration

Distinguish possibility, probability, tendency, limitation and evidential strength.

02

Nominalisation & density

Compress information without producing unreadable noun stacks.

03

Complex syntax

Control concession, condition, subordination, attachment and punctuation.

04

Paraphrase & synthesis

Preserve logic while changing structure, emphasis and lexical choices.

05

Stance & evaluation

Signal agreement, reservation, evidential limits and proportional criticism.

06

Cohesion & information flow

Use reference, lexical chains and thematic progression rather than connector overload.

A demanding weekly C1/C2 resource routine

Reading · 2 × 30 min

Read outside your field. Write a 60-word synthesis, one limitation and one supported inference that is not explicitly stated.

Listening · 2 × 30 min

Listen once without a transcript. Reconstruct the lecture map: thesis, concessions, examples, correction and conclusion. Then verify.

Speaking · 3 × 10 min

Record one 60-second answer. Repeat it twice: first for clearer structure, then for better stress, linking and lexical precision.

Writing · 2 × 20 min

Rewrite one paragraph for scope, stance and compression. Remove one overclaim, one vague reference and one unnecessary connector.

TEST DAY

Protect your performance

Exact procedures depend on your test mode and can change. Your ETS appointment information and official instructions always take priority.

48 hours before

  • Verify the time, location and required identification.
  • Check technical requirements if you are taking the Home Edition.
  • Sleep normally; do not replace preparation with an all-night session.
  • Do one short, controlled practice session rather than a full marathon.

During the test

  • Do not chase every unknown word; protect the structure of your understanding.
  • In multistage Reading/Listening, treat every item as consequential.
  • In Speaking, state the answer early and make the hierarchy of ideas audible.
  • In Writing, reserve a final check for logic, sentence boundaries and word form.

C-level discipline

  • Avoid claims stronger than the evidence.
  • Prefer exact wording to ornamental vocabulary.
  • Use complexity only when you can control it.
  • Make contrast, concession and conclusion unmistakable.
